# SPECIFIC AIMS

## TRACE-ESUS: Transparent and Reproducible AI for Causal Endotyping in ESUS

> **Evidence-checked working copy — 2026-08-06.** Unresolved fields are explicitly marked and must not be converted into guessed values. This copy does not alter the Google Drive source.

Cryptogenic stroke and embolic stroke of undetermined source (ESUS) conceal biologically distinct causes. Four major trials of anticoagulation in ESUS or atrial cardiopathy did not demonstrate overall superiority over antiplatelet therapy; ARCADIA uniquely enriched patients using electrical, biochemical, or structural atrial-cardiopathy markers. We hypothesize that mechanistic dilution contributes to these neutral effects: atrial disease, structural remodeling, systemic prothrombotic processes, renal or heart-failure-related biomarker elevation, and competing vascular sources can produce overlapping measurements while differing in causal relevance. Conventional clustering identifies resemblance but does not by itself determine which mechanism generated the evidence, whether that evidence would persist if a candidate mechanism were disabled, or whether the inference will survive changing assays, missingness, and comorbidity prevalence.

Our preliminary studies establish feasibility and sharpen the central question. Under strong renal distortion, a biology-constrained structural causal model maintained 81.9% true-mechanism ranking accuracy and 18.5% false atrial attribution, whereas pooled association fell to 57.9% and 76.0%; a correctly renal-adjusted comparator nearly matched the causal model, showing that explicit nuisance modeling, rather than causal labeling alone, drove this gain. In homogeneous single-mechanism cohorts, pooled association selected two endotypes in 500/500 replicates, whereas nuisance-adjusted and biology-constrained models retained one. Under isolated renal shift, pooled association lost 3.90 percentage points of accuracy versus 0.03 for the modular causal model; under combined shift, limited recalibration reduced false atrial attribution to 22.5% versus 44.7%.

In a decision-grade ARCADIA feasibility simulation anchored to the individual-participant trial data (N=1,015; 507/508 randomized; 80 recurrent strokes; 71 events by the prespecified two-year horizon; 919/1,015 [90.5%] jointly observed for NT-proBNP, PTFV1, and left atrial diameter), the posterior-score interaction test maintained approximately 4.5%–5.3% type-I error and 94.7%–95.5% confidence-interval coverage across K=2–5, but had only 6.4%–9.3% power under the prespecified moderate-effect scenario. Even the most optimistic tested K=2 scenario reached only 18.0% power. These results support exploratory frozen-score evaluation and event-requirement planning in ARCADIA; they do **not** establish that real-data causal endotyping has outperformed an associative comparator. Thus, neither association alone nor an unvalidated frozen causal graph is sufficient.

Our long-term goal is mechanism-guided secondary stroke prevention. TRACE-ESUS will develop, biologically validate, and transport an outcome-blind causal-AI framework that separates stable atrial-thromboembolic mechanisms from adaptable nuisance, measurement, and cohort processes. We hypothesize that modular causal separation will improve reliable inference and transportability, and that counterfactual sufficiency and disablement will add incremental value when multiple mechanisms can generate the same evidence.

**Aim 1. Define the identifiability, incremental value, and operating boundaries of modular counterfactual endotyping.** Using synthetic and ARCADIA-calibrated semi-synthetic cohorts with known mechanism truth, we will compare clinical rules, adjusted association, target calibration, frozen and modular causal models, posterior versus counterfactual querying of the same fitted model, and the full uncertainty-aware framework. Experiments will test component-specific gain under overlap, nuisance distortion, informative missingness, graph error, and distribution shift. Outcomes will include mechanism recovery, false atrial attribution, calibration, K=1 false discovery, uncertainty coverage, transport degradation, and selective risk. We will define when simpler adjustment is sufficient, when modular causal inference adds value, and when evidence is non-identifying. The product will be a frozen method, open software, and an operating-characteristic atlas.

**Aim 2. Derive and independently replicate outcome-blind causal mechanism fingerprints across ARCADIA and Brown/Rhode Island.** In ARCADIA (N=1,015), treatment and recurrent-stroke outcomes will remain masked while we audit marker coverage; fit staged clinical, latent, and biology-constrained models; test stability, biological controls, graph sensitivity, and missing-not-at-random sensitivity; and lock one continuous atrial-thromboembolic score. Replication will test fingerprint equivalence and associations with held-out AF, cardiac phenotypes, imaging, or recurrent-stroke evidence. Unstable classes will become continuous axes; incomplete overlap will use an invariant core fingerprint with site-specific measurement modules. The product will be a cross-cohort-supported mechanism atlas and a final frozen score with calibrated uncertainty.
