# PART 2 — THE TECHNICAL STORY, IN THREE LAYERS

You'll need each concept at three depths depending on who's asking.

## Layer 1 — for Dr. Khan (clinical)

> **"A high NT-proBNP has more than one owner."**
>
> Say the patient's NT-proBNP is elevated. Atrial stretch explains it. So does their heart failure. So
> does their kidney disease. A standard model asks *which profile does this patient resemble* — and
> resemblance says atrial, because atrial patients have high NT-proBNP.
>
> What we do instead is ask two different questions. First: *if the atrial process were the only thing
> going on, would it produce these exact labs?* Second: *if we switched the atrial process off and left
> everything else alone, would the labs still be abnormal?*
>
> If the answer to the second is yes — the labs stay abnormal without the atrium — then we can't credit
> the atrium, even though the patient looks atrial. That's the case we're trying to catch, and it's the
> case where anticoagulation would be the wrong call.

Then the honest part:

> "Here's the problem I found. Our current simulation can't produce that situation. We built it so each
> patient has exactly one mechanism and nothing else can generate the same labs. So when you switch off
> the atrial process, the evidence always disappears — there's nothing left to explain it. We've been
> testing for a phenomenon our own test tube can't grow."

## Layer 2 — for Dr. Bukhari (methodological)

> "Sufficiency and disablement returned rankings identical to the posterior at every confounding level.
> But it's not an empirical null — it's algebraic. With K = 2 mutually exclusive mechanisms and Gaussian
> noise, all three scores are strictly monotone in the same log-odds statistic, so their argmaxes have to
> agree. The script's docstring says so explicitly.
>
> The binding premises are mutual exclusivity at K = 2 and the absence of a redundant generating path.
> Signature asymmetry doesn't break it — our λ vectors are already asymmetric, 2 markers versus 1, and it
> collapsed anyway.
>
> So I want to state it as a proposition — the continuous analogue of Richens' noisy-OR result — and then
> violate the premises one at a time. Redundant nuisance paths first, because that's the clinically real
> one and it's where disablement should decouple from the posterior."

## Layer 3 — the proposition itself

> Under K = 2 mutually exclusive mechanisms, complete evidence, additive homoscedastic Gaussian noise,
> and no non-mechanism path capable of generating a mechanism's full signature, expected sufficiency,
> expected disablement, and the posterior are strictly monotone transformations of a common scalar.
> Their argmaxes coincide, so top-1 accuracy and every ranking metric are identical.
>
> **Corollary:** any observed counterfactual advantage must come from violating a premise. That tells us
> exactly where to look.
